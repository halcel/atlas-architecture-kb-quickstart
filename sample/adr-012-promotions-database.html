ADR-012: Database for the promotions service

Status: Accepted · Owner: Retail Domain Architecture · Last reviewed: 2026-10-28

Context

The promotions service manages weekly offers, coupons and loyalty campaigns for roughly 3,000 stores. A promotion has a header (validity, channels, regions), a variable list of articles with prices, display texts per language and per channel, and store-specific overrides.

Today promotions are stored across nine relational tables. Reading one promotion for the app requires five joins, and every new channel (app, e-receipt, digital signage) has added columns or tables. The team spends most change requests on schema migrations instead of features.

Decision drivers

  • Read latency below 20 ms (p95) for "promotion by id" and "active promotions for store X", the two hottest access patterns.
  • Frequent schema evolution: new channels and attributes roughly every quarter.
  • Peak load on Monday mornings when the new promotion week goes live (about 40x normal reads).
  • Full-text and semantic search over promotion texts for the internal planning tool.
  • Operational simplicity: the team has two engineers on call and no dedicated DBA.

Considered options

Option A: Keep PostgreSQL, normalize further

Pros: team knows it well, strong consistency across tables, existing reporting jobs keep working.

Cons: joins grow with every channel; JSONB columns already used as a workaround for channel texts; search needs a separate engine or extension.

Option B: MongoDB Atlas, one document per promotion

Pros: the promotion is read and written as one document, matching how the app consumes it; new attributes need no migration; Atlas Search and Vector Search run on the same data; managed scaling for the Monday peak.

Cons: reporting jobs need to move to change streams or Atlas SQL; team needs enablement on document modelling.

Decision

We choose Option B, MongoDB Atlas. Each promotion is one document with embedded articles, channel texts and store overrides. Store overrides above 200 entries are moved to a separate collection to keep documents bounded.

Consequences

  • Reporting moves to an analytics node plus Atlas SQL within two sprints.
  • A JSON schema validator enforces required fields (validity, regions, at least one article).
  • Search for the planning tool uses Atlas Search; no separate search cluster.
  • Revisit if access patterns shift to many-to-many reporting across promotions, articles and stores.
